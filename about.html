About

NUB explains US personal finance and insurance in plain English — car and home insurance, credit, debt, savings, retirement accounts, taxes, and mortgages. The goal is simple: take the rules that actually govern your money and write down what they say, without jargon and without padding.

Who writes NUB

NUB is written by Daniel Han.

Daniel is not a licensed financial advisor, accountant, insurance agent, or attorney, and nothing published here is personalized advice. What he does is read the primary sources — IRS publications, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance, Social Security Administration rules, state insurance department materials, and the Insurance Information Institute — and explain what they actually say.

That distinction matters, so it is stated on every article as well as here. If you need someone to tell you what you should do, that requires a licensed professional who knows your full situation. What NUB can do is make sure you walk into that conversation understanding how the thing works.

How an article gets made

  1. Primary sources first. Every article starts from the agency or regulator that sets the rule, not from other blogs. Sources are listed and linked at the bottom of each article so you can check them yourself.
  2. Figures are verified on the day of publication. Contribution limits, deduction amounts, and coverage thresholds change every year. Any number that moves annually is checked against the issuing agency before the article goes live, and the applicable year is stated in the text.
  3. Scope is stated, not implied. Insurance and tax rules vary by state and by carrier. Where a rule is not uniform nationwide, the article says so instead of presenting one state's version as universal.
  4. Every money article carries a disclaimer. Not as boilerplate at the bottom, but near the top, where you will actually read it.
